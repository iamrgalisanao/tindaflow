# ADR-016: Configurable Meal Menu, Employee Credit, Supervisor Override, Slip Mode and Billing

## Status
Proposed — 2026-10-25 (design only; no code, migration or database change accompanies this ADR). Requested for the first canteen client (TSC, a staff canteen concessionaire). Extends ADR-003 (checkout transaction), ADR-006 (snapshots), ADR-010 (idempotency) and ADR-014 (locked fiscal configuration); amends ADR-012 only in that the unit price of a meal line comes from the menu, still computed server-side. Build order and estimates: [canteen-build-plan.md](../../06-backend/canteen-build-plan.md).

## Client decision on invoicing (recorded, not a requirement we derived)
TSC has decided to use TindaFlow as **internal sales monitoring only** and to issue its own invoices manually. That is the client's compliance decision. TindaFlow does not assert, and this ADR does not claim, any legal requirement about it. Consequences kept deliberately small: a store-level setting selects between the existing invoice path (unchanged) and a slip path; the slip carries a plain "not an invoice" line so staff and employees do not mistake it for one; changing the setting is held behind the same vendor unlock as other fiscal configuration (ADR-014) so a shop cannot flip it casually. Whether TSC's chosen mode is acceptable for its business is TSC's responsibility.

## Context
The current POS sells one catalog product per line, has one flat category level, always issues an invoice (needs a fiscal installation, invoice series, fiscal day and Z-reading), accepts CASH|GCASH|MAYA|CARD|OTHER, and has no employee or credit concept. The meal picker added in commit 5ec4c51 is screen-only: `mealMenus.js` maps proteins to products by SKU and the chosen dish is a cart note that never reaches the server, the receipt or a report. A later inventory phase will track cooked dishes per day, so a dish must be a real record with a stable id.

Existing facts this design must respect: sale lines are append-only snapshots (`sale_items`, `sale_items.product_id` is NOT NULL, ADR-006, invariants #2/#7); the database role `tindaflow_app` cannot UPDATE or DELETE append-only tables (`docker/README.md`, `tindaflow:harden-database`); `expected_cash` counts only `CASH` payments net of change (`ShiftReadingAggregator`); capabilities are a fixed code-level catalog with roles ADMIN/MANAGER/CASHIER (domain-model §2.2); stage 31 deliberately has no "manager PIN" mechanism; `GlobalLockOrder` is Shift, FiscalDay, Sale, SaleItem, InvoiceSeries; money is a `decimal(12,2)` handled by `Money`.

## Decision

### 1. Navigation: `menu_groups`, separate from `categories`
A two-level tree `menu_groups(id, store_id, parent_id NULL, name, sort_order, active)`. Seven top groups (Breakfast, Lunch, Snack/Merienda, Baked Products, Varieties, Beverages, Coffeeshop), sub-groups under Breakfast (Ala Carte, Silog Meal) and Lunch (Standard Meal, Combo Meal). `products.menu_group_id` (nullable) places a product; a meal is placed the same way (`meals.menu_group_id`). Depth is capped at two in the service. `categories` stay exactly as they are: they feed the gross-profit-by-category report and must not change meaning. Search by name or SKU stays one box above the tree and ignores the tree. SKU is already mandatory and unique per store.

### 2. Meal menu data model (configuration, mutable, never deleted)
| Table | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `dish_categories` | Meat/price category: Beef, Chicken, Pork, Fish, Vegetable, Side. `code`, `name`, `sort_order`, `active`. |
| `dishes` | A dish record with its own id: `name`, `dish_category_id`, `sort_order`, `active` (hide, never delete). Renaming is an UPDATE plus an audit event. The future inventory phase attaches here. |
| `meals` | A sellable meal: Standard Meal, Combo Meal. `menu_group_id`, `name`, `backing_product_id` (see 3), `sort_order`, `active`. |
| `meal_option_groups` | `meal_id`, `name`, `kind` CHOICE or ADDON, `min_select`, `max_select`, `prices_the_meal` (exactly one CHOICE group per meal is true), `step_order`, `popup_after_choice` (the rice pop-up). |
| `meal_options` | `option_group_id`, and either `dish_id` (CHOICE) or `label` + `addon_product_id` (ADDON), `sort_order`, `active`. |
| `menu_prices` | Append-only, effective-dated: `(meal_id, dish_category_id)` for a meal's price, or `meal_option_id` for an add-on; `amount`, `effective_from`, `changed_by`, `reason`. The price in force at `sold_at` is the row with the latest `effective_from <= sold_at`. Rows are only inserted, so the table is the price change history. |

Worked configuration. Standard Meal: one CHOICE group "Viand" (min 1, max 1, `prices_the_meal`) of dishes; the picker shows step 1 (dish category) then step 2 (dish) by grouping dishes on `dish_category_id`, so the existing two-step interaction is kept without a tree. Prices: Beef 68; Chicken, Pork, Fish 60; Vegetable 60. Combo Meal: CHOICE group "Main" (1..1, `prices_the_meal`) and CHOICE group "Side" (1..1, Side-category dishes, no price); prices per main category 80/85 until the client confirms. Add-on group "Extra rice" with options Whole rice 15 and Half rice 10, `popup_after_choice` so it appears automatically after the viand is chosen and can be skipped (min 0). Price is by meat category, not by dish, as required: changing Beef from 68 to 70 is one new `menu_prices` row.

Admin screen: supervisor/manager (`CATALOG_MANAGE`, already held by MANAGER and ADMIN) builds dishes, meals, groups, options and prices; every change writes an audit event (`MENU_DISH_CREATED|RENAMED|HIDDEN`, `MENU_PRICE_CHANGED`, `MENU_STRUCTURE_CHANGED`). Menu structure edits are allowed mid-day; they affect only the next sale.

### 3. Sale-line snapshot and server-side resolution
A meal line must still be a `sale_items` row (reports, refunds, stock hooks, tax all key on it). Each meal has a hidden **backing product** (e.g. SKU `MEAL-STD`, `track_inventory = false`, tax class from the store) used for FK, tax class and product reports; its `selling_price` is not used for a meal line.

Checkout accepts, instead of a price, `items[].meal = {meal_id, choices: [{group_id, option_id}], addons: [option_id]}`. The server validates (options belong to the meal, are active, counts within min/max, quantity is 1 per meal line because each plate has its own dish), derives the meal price from `menu_prices` at `sold_at` using the option in the `prices_the_meal` group, and feeds it to `FinancialCalculator` as the unit price (ADR-012 holds: the client never supplies money). Each add-on becomes its **own sale line** on its backing product (its own price from `menu_prices`, refundable on its own) marked with the parent line.

New append-only snapshot storage, written in the same transaction:
- `sale_items` gains nullable `menu_group_path_snapshot` (e.g. `Lunch > Standard Meal`; also filled for plain products so billing can group by meal type), `meal_id`, `meal_name_snapshot`, `parent_line_number`.
- `sale_item_selections(id, sale_item_id, group_name_snapshot, option_label_snapshot, dish_id, dish_name_snapshot, dish_category_snapshot, price_snapshot, sort_order)`. `dish_id` is a restrict FK kept for the inventory phase; reports and slips read the `*_snapshot` columns only.

Renaming or hiding a dish, or changing a price, therefore cannot alter any past sale, slip or billing statement. The one-line picker in `mealMenus.js`/`MealPicker.jsx` is replaced by a picker driven by `GET /menu` (the active tree, meals, groups, options and current prices, cached on the till and re-fetched at shift open); an inactive or changed option is rejected or re-priced by the server and the till shows the returned total.

### 4. Payment methods
`payments.method` and `refund_settlements.payment_method` CHECK constraints gain `BANK_TRANSFER` and `CREDIT`. Bank transfer by QR is an ordinary non-cash tender (the cashier photographs the customer's phone outside the system). `payments.reference_note` already exists and checkout already accepts `external_reference`; the only work is showing an **optional** reference field for BANK_TRANSFER, GCASH and MAYA in `TenderPanel`. Never mandatory.

### 5. Employees and credit
**Employees** (`employees`, mutable lifecycle table): `store_id`, `employee_type` DIRECT|SUBCONTRACTOR, `employee_code` (system generated), `name`, `billing_party_id`, `active`. `employee_code` = `TSC` + 5 digits for DIRECT, `SUB` + 5 digits for SUBCONTRACTOR, from a per-store per-type counter row locked FOR UPDATE (`employee_code_sequences`), never reused, UNIQUE(store_id, employee_code), immutable (column-level GRANT leaves it out of the app role's UPDATE list). The QR value is the code itself; the code carries no personal data. Lookup is by scanner (keyboard-wedge text) or typed reference number, exact match. New capability `EMPLOYEE_MANAGE` (create, edit, deactivate, bulk CSV import, card print) is granted to ADMIN only; the cashier has only the lookup that returns name, code, type, active and credit status. There is no create path from the till.

**Billing parties** (`billing_parties`: `name`, `employee_type`, `active`), seeded with "TSC" (DIRECT) and "Subcontractors" (SUBCONTRACTOR). It exists so that SUB employees belonging to several subcontractor companies can be billed to the right one later without a schema change; today it is two rows.

**Credit tender.** `CREDIT` is a payment method on a payments row: the whole sale, a single payment equal to the grand total (no splitting with cash for now; an open item). It is a tender the cashier selects and then confirms; there is no amount entry, change or payment step. Because it is a normal payments row, invariant #8 (payments cover the total) holds unchanged. `sales` gains nullable `employee_id` so the employee is on the sale, not in a note.

**Credit ledger** `employee_credit_entries` (append-only): `employee_id`, `entry_type` CHARGE|REVERSAL, `amount` (positive), `period_start` DATE, `sale_id`, `refund_id`/`void_id` (for reversals), `override_grant_id` NULL, plus snapshots `employee_code`, `employee_name`, `employee_type`, `billing_party_id` (so billing never reads the mutable employee row for history). Balance for a period = SUM(CHARGE) - SUM(REVERSAL) for that `period_start`; nothing stores a running balance.

**Cut-off.** Two periods per month: the 1st to the 15th, and the 16th to the **last day of the month** (28, 29, 30 or 31). The client said "16 to 30"; that would leave the 31st in no period, so the end of month is used and this is listed for the client to confirm. The period is computed from `sold_at` in `Asia/Manila` (the app timezone) and stored on the ledger entry. A refund or void reverses against the **original charge's** period (reducing what is owed for that period, not freeing room in the current one).

**Limit.** Default PHP 1,500 per cut-off per employee, held in `credit_policies(store_id, employee_type, limit_per_cutoff)` so SUB can differ without code. Check, inside the checkout transaction: `period balance + this charge <= limit`, else refuse with `CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409, carrying the balance and remaining headroom) unless a valid override is presented (section 6). A preview endpoint (`GET /employees/{id}/credit-status`) lets the till warn the cashier before checkout; the warning is advisory, the checkout check is authoritative.

**Concurrency (two tills).** Checkout locks the employee row `FOR UPDATE` before reading the period balance. `LockableResource` gains `Employee`, ordered between FiscalDay and Sale (Shift 0, FiscalDay 1, **Employee 2**, Sale 3, SaleItem 4, InvoiceSeries 5). Void and refund of a credit sale read the employee id from the immutable payments/sales row, lock the employee first, then the sale, so the order is the same on every path. The second till waits, then sees the first till's charge. A database-level backstop is not possible for a time-windowed sum, so the lock is the control and a two-connection test in `tests/Database` is mandatory.

**Refund and void of a credit sale** reduce what is owed, never cash. The refund settlement method is `CREDIT`; it writes a REVERSAL ledger entry in the original period and writes nothing to cash. `ShiftReadingAggregator` already counts only `CASH` settlements as cash refunds, and a test pins that credit sales add nothing to `cash_sales`, `expected_cash` or change allocation. Credit shows as its own line in the reading (a non-cash tender) so shift totals still foot.

### 6. Supervisor override at the till
New capability `CREDIT_LIMIT_OVERRIDE` (MANAGER and ADMIN). The client says "supervisor/manager"; V1 has no SUPERVISOR role and no dynamic roles (domain-model §2.2), so a supervisor is a MANAGER. This is the first approver-at-the-till mechanism; stage 31's "no manager PIN" stays true for discounts and everything else.

- **Code.** `user_override_codes(user_id PK, code_hash, failed_attempts, locked_until, set_at, set_by)`: a 6 to 8 digit code the approver sets for themselves from their own signed-in session (password re-entered), or an ADMIN resets. It must differ from the login password. Stored with `Hash::make` (Argon2id/bcrypt via the framework hasher), never reversible, never logged. It is separate from login, so a leaked override code does not open the back office.
- **Approver is named, not discovered.** The till shows the store's eligible approvers; the approver taps their name and types their code. A code-only lookup would require a searchable deterministic hash of a 6-digit secret, which a database leak would make trivial to brute-force; named approver plus a slow hash plus per-user lockout does not.
- **Lockout.** 5 wrong codes lock that approver's override for 15 minutes (`locked_until`), plus a throttle per terminal (10 attempts per 15 minutes). Verification runs **in its own committed transaction**, not inside checkout, because a failure counter written inside a rolled-back checkout would be lost and the lockout would be bypassable.
- **Per-sale grant.** A successful verification inserts a `credit_override_grants` row and returns a random token (stored hashed): bound to `terminal_id`, `employee_id`, the cashier, a ceiling `max_charge` (the charge being approved), expiring in 5 minutes, single use. Checkout presents the token; inside the transaction, under the employee lock, it atomically marks the grant consumed (`UPDATE ... WHERE consumed_at IS NULL`, one row affected), checks binding and ceiling, and only then allows the charge over the limit. The token is consumed only when the limit would actually be exceeded. It authorizes one sale, never a session, never "raise the limit".
- **Audit.** Every event writes an audit row with approver, cashier, terminal, employee, amount, balance and limit at the time: `CREDIT_OVERRIDE_GRANTED`, `CREDIT_OVERRIDE_USED` (with the sale id, written in the sale's transaction), `CREDIT_OVERRIDE_REFUSED` (wrong code, locked, expired, replayed, wrong binding), `OVERRIDE_CODE_SET`, `OVERRIDE_CODE_RESET`. The ledger entry carries `override_grant_id`, so any over-limit charge links to its approver.
- **Approving your own use.** A MANAGER cashier approving their own over-limit credit sale is refused (approver id must differ from cashier id).

### 7. Slip mode (store-level `sale_document_mode`)
`store_settings.sale_document_mode` = `INVOICE` (default; every existing store, path untouched) or `SLIP`. Changing it requires `FISCAL_CONFIGURATION_MANAGE` (the ADR-014 unlock), is refused while any shift or fiscal day in the store is open, and writes `SALE_DOCUMENT_MODE_CHANGED`. Each `sales` row records `document_mode` (NOT NULL, default `INVOICE`) at finalization, so a store that switches has an unambiguous history.

In SLIP mode `CheckoutService` skips the fiscal installation resolver, the invoice series allocator and the invoice insert, and instead, in the same transaction, inserts one `sale_slips` row: `sale_id` UNIQUE, `terminal_id`, `slip_number`, `slip_snapshot_json`, `created_at`. `slip_number` is a running integer **per terminal** from `slip_counters(terminal_id, last_number)`, incremented inside the checkout transaction under the shift lock already held (a rolled-back sale leaves no gap), shown as `<terminal code>-<6 digits>`. It is a separate sequence from `invoice_series`, never prefixed or formatted like an invoice number, and exists for audit. No fiscal journal entry is written for a slip (the electronic journal remains an invoice-mode artifact); the `SALE_FINALIZED` audit event is still written with the slip number.

The slip is very small (thermal paper is costly): store name, slip number, date-time, cashier; for credit the employee name and code; meal and dish (and add-ons) from the selection snapshot; total; payment type and optional reference; a one-line "Internal slip, not an invoice". No tax breakdown, buyer block, TIN or series data. Printed through the existing ADR-007 pipeline; every sale, cash and credit, prints one; a reprint reads `slip_snapshot_json`.

Shift and day behavior in SLIP mode:
- **Shift open/close, cash count, X-reading: unchanged.** Opening cash, declared cash, variance, cash in/out and the X-reading (an internal shift report) all stay. This is the reconciliation the client needs, and credit sales stay out of it.
- **Fiscal day and Z-reading: kept structurally, relabelled.** `sales.fiscal_day_id` is NOT NULL and invariants #35, #36 and #42 tie every sale, shift and reading to the day; removing it is a rewrite with no benefit. In SLIP mode the "fiscal day" is simply the business day (it already opens automatically with the first shift and needs no fiscal installation) and its Z-reading is an **internal end-of-day report**: same totals, no PTU/series lines, title "End of day report", ZReading permission and printing unchanged. Cashier closes shifts; a manager closes the day. Whether the client wants any end-of-day totals at all is an open item; nothing forces them to print it.
- **Out of scope:** any invoice, official-receipt, BIR/CAS/CRM/PTU behavior in SLIP mode, fiscal journal output, invoice reprint, VAT breakdown. The tax registration row is still read by the calculator (NON_VAT is a supported registration type); which one the client wants is an open item.
- **Void and refund in SLIP mode** work on the sale as today but print/reference the slip number; code paths that assume an invoice row (listed in the build plan) get a document-agnostic reference.

### 8. Billing
Billing is a **report over the credit ledger**, not a second source of truth.
- **Generator.** Inputs: party (or employee type), a date range or a named cut-off (period shortcuts for the 1st-15th / 16th-end), "as of" defaults to now. Re-runnable at any date: it recomputes from the ledger (like readings, invariant #40), so an old range re-run later shows the current net, with late refunds/voids included and marked as adjustments.
- **Layout.** Billing party, then Account name (the employee), then Meal type (`menu_group_path_snapshot`), then Item/dish (selection snapshot; add-ons under their parent's meal type). Employee totals, party total. TSC and SUB are separate runs because they are separate parties. "Account name" is read as the employee; to be confirmed.
- **Saved run (optional).** `billing_runs` (append-only: number `BR-000123`, party, range, generated_by, `totals_snapshot` jsonb) is a frozen copy for cheque reference. A later re-run compares against it and shows the difference; no saved run is ever edited.
- **Cheques.** `billing_cheques` (party, cheque number, bank, cheque date, received date, amount, recorded_by, status RECEIVED|RETURNED, only `status`/`returned_at` updatable) and append-only `billing_cheque_allocations(cheque_id, period_start, amount)`. Owed per party and period = ledger net - allocations. Allocations cannot exceed the cheque amount (checked under a lock on the cheque). Recording a cheque is not a cash movement and never touches a shift. `REPORT_VIEW` reads billing; a new `BILLING_MANAGE` (ADMIN and MANAGER) records cheques and saves runs. Cheque recording goes through the idempotency service (two new operation types).

### 9. Hardening and migrations
Every new table is created by the owner role in a migration. In `harden_append_only_privileges.sql` (and `AppendOnlyPrivilegesTest`): append-only (REVOKE UPDATE) `menu_prices`, `sale_item_selections`, `sale_slips`, `employee_credit_entries`, `billing_runs`, `billing_cheque_allocations`; lifecycle with column-level UPDATE only: `credit_override_grants` (`consumed_at`), `billing_cheques` (`status`, `returned_at`), `employees` (not `employee_code`/`employee_type`), `user_override_codes` (the three counter columns), `slip_counters`, `employee_code_sequences` (counters). Configuration tables (`menu_groups`, `dish_categories`, `dishes`, `meals`, `meal_option_groups`, `meal_options`, `billing_parties`, `credit_policies`) allow UPDATE but, like everything else, never DELETE. The DELETE allow-list in `HardenDatabase` does not change (no new deletable tables); its read-back of the append-only set must include the new tables.

## Alternatives considered
- **Keep the picker screen-only and add the dish as a text note on the line.** Rejected: the dish would not be a record, so no billing, no reports by dish, and no inventory attach point.
- **One product per meal-and-protein-and-dish combination (hundreds of SKUs).** Rejected: price by meat category means one price change touches dozens of products, and "rename a dish" has no home.
- **Reuse `categories` for the 7 groups and sub-groups.** Rejected for now: the gross-profit-by-category report and product CSV depend on a flat category; navigation and accounting classification are different jobs.
- **Make `sale_items.product_id` nullable for meals.** Rejected: weakens an FK that reports and refunds rely on; a hidden backing product is cheaper and reversible.
- **Treat credit as "no payment" (a sale without a payments row).** Rejected: breaks invariant #8, the payment breakdown, and refund settlement symmetry. A `CREDIT` payment row keeps all of it.
- **A running balance column on `employees`.** Rejected: mutable money that must be kept right; the ledger sum under a row lock is exact and auditable.
- **Override code verified inside the checkout request.** Rejected: failed-attempt counters would roll back with the failed transaction, so lockout would not work. Separate grant plus single-use token fixes it.
- **Code-only approver (no name selection).** Rejected: needs a deterministic searchable hash of a short secret.
- **Reuse `DISCOUNT_OVERRIDE`/capability for the override.** Rejected: the override is exercised from a different person at someone else's till, which a capability check on the acting user cannot express.
- **Make slip mode a fiscal-installation type or a terminal flag.** Rejected: the mode is a business decision of the store and must be uniform across its tills so mixed documents cannot occur; a flag per terminal invites exactly that.
- **Delete the fiscal day / Z-reading in slip mode.** Rejected: structural coupling (sales, shifts, invariants) with no benefit; relabelling is enough.
- **A global (store-wide) slip counter.** Rejected: serializes every till on one row; per-terminal is already audit-sufficient.

## Consequences
Easier: menu, price and dish changes need no deploy; reports and billing can group by dish; the future inventory phase attaches to `dishes.id` and `sale_item_selections.dish_id`; the invoice path is untouched for stores that need it; every over-limit credit sale names its approver.

Harder: checkout grows a meal-resolution step and an employee lock; the lock order changes (documented, tested); roughly 18 backend and 9 frontend files that assume an invoice row (sales list/detail, void, refund, reprint, lookup, sales and void/refund reports) need a document-agnostic reference; the QR value is the guessable sequential code, so anyone who knows `TSC00012` can charge that employee. Mitigated, not eliminated, by the slip naming the employee at the till, per-employee limits, and billing review before payroll deduction; a secret in the QR is a future option. Accepted. The override code is a short secret typed at a shared till; the lockout, named approver, per-sale scope and audit limit the damage, they do not make shoulder-surfing impossible.

Out of scope: dish inventory and daily reset, baked-goods shelf life, a per-store configurable role model, mixed cash and credit tender, installments or payroll-deduction files, employee self-service, any invoice or fiscal behavior in slip mode.

## Amendment 2026-10-25: slice 2.2a (slip mode core) as built

Recorded where the build differs from, or settles, the text above. Nothing here changes the client decision on invoicing.

1. **The slip never says "invoice".** Section 7 listed a one-line "Internal slip, not an invoice". The client's final instruction is that a slip must not use the words invoice or official receipt at all, and that the closing line is the store's own to word. So the slip carries no such line: a store setting `store_settings.slip_footer` (free text, up to 200 characters, empty by default, `STORE_SETTINGS_MANAGE`) is printed at the bottom when set. The footer is copied into the slip's snapshot at the sale, like everything else on it.
2. **Tables.** `sale_slips` is as designed plus `store_id`, `slip_sequence` (the integer behind the number) and unique `(store_id, slip_number)` and `(terminal_id, slip_sequence)`; `slip_counters` is `(terminal_id PK, last_number, timestamps)`. The `slip_snapshot_json` (schema_version 1) holds store name, slip number, date-time, cashier name, the lines (description from the sale line, `is_addon` from `parent_line_number`), discount, total, each payment with its optional reference, tendered and change, and the footer.
3. **Original and copies mirror the invoice rules, not the Z-reading's.** `GET /sale-slips/{id}` is always the plain original; `POST /sale-slips/{id}/reprints` is the only way to print a marked copy and records a `SLIP_REPRINTED` audit event (idempotent via the new `SLIP_REPRINT` operation type). As with `invoiceGet`, nothing stops someone calling the plain GET again, so an unmarked duplicate is possible through the API; the till and the lookup panel never offer it. If the client wants the Z-reading's server-claimed original, that is a unique `sale_slip_prints` row and a small change.
4. **Mode switch is its own resource.** `GET /sale-document-settings`, `PUT /sale-document-settings/mode` (`FISCAL_CONFIGURATION_MANAGE`) and `PUT /sale-document-settings/slip-footer` (`STORE_SETTINGS_MANAGE`), rather than extra fields on `PATCH /store-settings`, so the existing StoreSettings contract and its tests are unchanged. The switch takes a row lock on the store; `ShiftOpenService` takes a shared lock on the same row, so a shift cannot open between the "nothing is open" check and the switch. A store with no `store_settings` row gets one on its first switch (its own name, blank registered name and TIN, the same baseline the business details screen shows).
5. **Lock order.** `LockableResource::SlipCounter` (order 5) is taken last, after Shift, and never in the same operation as InvoiceSeries. When slice 3.2 inserts Employee at order 2, SlipCounter becomes 6.
6. **Tax in slip mode (open item 7).** The tax registration is still read. A registration that exists is used exactly as in invoice mode (product classes must be compatible with it). A store with **no** registration is computed as `NON_VAT` with every line classified `NON_VAT`: no VAT is invented, `sales.non_vat_sales` equals the total, and the `SALE_FINALIZED` audit event carries `tax_registration_assumed: NON_VAT`. Readiness does not require a tax registration, fiscal installation or invoice series in slip mode. Which treatment the canteen's accountant wants remains open item 7.
7. **What slip mode still writes.** Shift opened/closed, X-reading and Z-reading journal entries, and the `VOID`/`REFUND` journal entries of a slip sale, are unchanged (they are keyed to the shift, reading, void or refund, not to an invoice); the void and refund payloads carry `slip_number` and a null `invoice_number`. Only the sale's own `INVOICE` journal entry and the invoice row are skipped.
8. **End-of-day report.** Whether a day's Z-reading is labelled "end of day report" is derived from the `document_mode` of the day's sales (the mode cannot change while a day is open); a day with no sale follows the store's current mode. The stored totals are untouched; the printed report in slip mode drops the tax lines, the reset counter and the accumulated-sales counters.

## Amendment 2026-10-25: slice 3.1 (employees and lookup) as built

Recorded where the build differs from, or settles, section 5. Nothing here adds credit tender, the ledger or the limit check (slices 3.2 and 3.3).

1. **One billing party.** The client resolved open item 4 (2026-10-25): subcontractor employees are billed to TSC as well. `billing_parties` therefore has no `employee_type` and no second seeded row; each store has exactly one party named TSC (the migration creates it for existing stores, `BillingPartyResolver` for a new one on first use). Both employee types point at it. The table stays so a second party needs no schema change.
2. **`credit_policies` is a mutable current value, not history.** One row per `(store_id, employee_type)` with `limit_per_cutoff` (TSC 1,500.00 and SUB 1,000.00 by default, client 2026-10-25), editable by `EMPLOYEE_MANAGE` and audited as `CREDIT_LIMIT_CHANGED` with the old and new value. Effective-dated rows were rejected: the limit is only ever compared with the present (the checkout check of slice 3.2), never used to recompute a past balance, and the append-only audit event plus the limit recorded on each override already answer "what was the limit then". Rows are created by the migration for existing stores and lazily (insert-or-ignore) for a new store, on the first policy read or first employee. Column-level UPDATE: `limit_per_cutoff`, `updated_by`, `updated_at`.
3. **Names are three columns.** `first_name`, `middle_name` (optional), `last_name` instead of one `name`; the joined "First Middle Last" is what the till, slip and ID card show. Type values are the ADR's `DIRECT` and `SUBCONTRACTOR`; the screens and the CSV also say TSC and SUB.
4. **Manager does not hold `EMPLOYEE_MANAGE`.** ADMIN only, exactly as section 5 says (a manager is the credit supervisor of slice 3.3, not the person who enrols staff). Reads are gated too: there is no listing for a cashier or manager, only `employeeLookup`.
5. **Lookup** is `GET /employees/lookup?code=`: session only, exact match after trimming whitespace and upper-casing, store-scoped, returns id, code, type, name, active. Unknown is `EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND` (404), deactivated is `EMPLOYEE_INACTIVE` (409). A bare number is not accepted (it would be ambiguous between TSC and SUB). The credit status joins this response in slice 3.2.
6. **CSV import** takes `first_name,middle_name,last_name,employee_type`, creates employees with generated codes, and reports row errors. Re-importing creates new employees EXCEPT an exact duplicate (same type, same three names, ignoring case and spaces) of an existing employee (active or not) or of an earlier row of the file, which is skipped and reported. Two different people who really share a name are added one at a time. `?dry_run=true` previews the codes.
7. **Codes and locks.** The counter row `(store_id, employee_type)` is locked `FOR UPDATE` inside the creating transaction; the import takes both types' rows first, DIRECT then SUBCONTRACTOR, so two imports cannot deadlock. These locks are not in the checkout path and `LockableResource` is untouched; slice 3.2 still inserts Employee at order 2.
8. **ID cards print the code as text.** No QR library is installed and dependencies may not be added without approval, so the card sheet shows the name, type and the code in large type (the QR value is the code). A QR image on the card is an open item: it needs a small QR library (or a vetted in-house encoder) and an owner decision to add it. Until then the code is keyed in at the till; a keyboard-wedge barcode/QR scanner can only be used once the image exists.
